# Wishlist of machines

Every machine hako-devices wants to draw, in build order. The data is in [`data/wishlist.json`](../data/wishlist.json), one entry per machine. Names are descriptive only: no logos, no trademarks in the drawings. Checked on 2026-10-30.

**1408 machines. 661 distinct shapes still to build. 104 entries are already built: the 55 drawings in hotaru, plus the real models those meshes cover. 532 entries have a sourced size; the other 876 are `null` on purpose.**

The shape count is the real work: a group of machines that share a body is one mesh. It counts each `formFactorGroup` that has a planned machine and no built mesh.

## How to read the data

- `dimensionsMm` is `[w, d, h]` in millimetres, with the machine the way it is used. On a desk: `w` across, `d` front to back, `h` up. A phone, tablet or handheld held in front of you: `w` across the face, `d` the thickness, `h` up the face. A closed laptop: `w` across, `d` front to back, `h` the thickness. Notes say when something is measured folded, standing or with parts attached.
- A size from a source that gave inches is converted and rounded to 0.1 mm, so it can sit a millimetre off the metric figure.
- `dimensionsSource` is the page the size was read from. `null` size means no source was used: nothing here is guessed.
- `formFactorGroup` names the body. It is a first pass, made from model names and, for old Macs, the form factor on Apple's pages. Machines with the same outside size can still differ in ports or cameras; look before sharing a mesh. Groups in phases 1 and 2 are judged from the family and any published sizes.
- `status` is `built` for hotaru's drawings (phase 6, with their hotaru key) and for real models a built mesh covers (phase 4, noted). Generic drawings such as `pc-tower` and `client-browser` are in phase 6 only because they exist; the brief says generic PCs are not wanted.
- `notes` flags anything unconfirmed. Search for "unconfirmed" and "not confirmed".

## Sources and their limits

**Apple sizes: one decision for the owner.** Apple's spec pages are the best source for its machines: names, years and sizes for almost everything back to 1984. But [Apple's site terms](https://www.apple.com/legal/internet-services/terms/site.html) say no content may be copied or republished, and that data sheets are for personal, non-commercial use and not to be copied or posted on a networked computer. This package is public, so Apple's figures are **not** in this file. Apple's pages were read to check names, years and Wikipedia's figures, and their addresses are in `notes`. Sizes in the file come from Wikipedia. Whether single published measurements may be used is the owner's call. If yes, the gap fills in one run.

Apple in this file: 780 machines, 290 with a Wikipedia size, 490 without.

## Where this is incomplete

- **Years:** 66 entries carry a year that was not found in a source, or none. Most are BlackBerry, Siemens and Ericsson phones with no article of their own, Palm variants, and some older Apple displays. Those years came from memory and are flagged in `notes`. Years for Macs with no year on Apple's page were read from the Wikipedia list of Mac models by eye; they are not flagged.
- **Sizes for phases 1 to 3 are thin.** Palm m100 and Zire families, most Treo models, early BlackBerrys, Game & Watch (none at all), NES, Super NES, Genesis, the original PlayStation, Xbox 360, Virtual Boy, the Atari 2600 and most home computers have no size here. Wikipedia gave none and no other reusable source was found.
- **A family article's size is applied to its models.** Where an article gives one size for a series, the models it names share it. Where Apple's page disagreed by more than 2 mm for a model, the note says so, or the size was dropped (PowerBook 180c, 190cs, Duo 270c, 280, 280c, 2300c).
- **Axes:** many Wikipedia sizes have no labels. Those are assigned by shape and say so in `notes`. A few console sizes differ between an article's infobox and its text (32X, PlayStation 3 Slim); notes name them.
- **Not listed from Apple:** printers, scanners, cameras, keyboards, mice, AirPods, AirTag, Pencil, docks and adapters. Apple's older monitors are listed by name only.
- **Consoles:** body variants are listed where they are well known, not exhaustively: Genesis has three models here, but regional and third-party ones (Aiwa, Pioneer, Hi-Saturn, V-Saturn), PlayStation 2 slim revisions, Xbox and Wii U revisions, and the mini Famicom are left out or folded into their parent. Colour variants and special editions are not separate.
- **Phones:** "the famous ones" is a judgement. Nokia, Motorola, Sony Ericsson, Siemens and Ericsson are selections, not complete lines. Palm and Handspring are close to complete, Sidekick is complete per its article's table.
- **Home computers:** a selection, as asked. MSX is a standard with many makers; six machines stand in for it.
- **Very new machines** (iPhone Duo, iPhone 18 Pro, Mac mini M6, Surface Laptop Ultra, MacBook Pro with PROLED, and others) come from live pages on the check date. Some were announced and not yet shipping.
- **Surface:** Sizes come from Wikipedia's model tables; Microsoft's own figures were not read. Surface Laptop 6 and 7, the 15-inch Laptops and the 15-inch Books have no size here: those table cells hold two sizes and were not split. Surface Pro 12-inch and Laptop 13-inch are named on Microsoft Learn but not in Wikipedia's table. Surface Neo was announced and never sold.
